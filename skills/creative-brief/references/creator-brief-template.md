# Creator brief template

For a creator or partner making the ad. Keep it short and friendly; a creator needs a clear target and room to be themselves.

## Product
What it is, who it is for, and one sentence on why it matters. Include any product the creator must have in hand.

## Talking points
Three at most, in the order they matter. Say they are to be put in the creator's own words.

## Must say
Exact wording only where the brand or the law requires it (for example an approved claim). Mark approved claims and who approved them.

## Must avoid
Claims the brand cannot substantiate, competitor names, restricted topics, anything the platform's ad policies forbid. For regulated categories (health, finance, alcohol, weight loss and similar), list what must not be implied.

## Deliverables and specs
How many videos or images, length, aspect ratios (9:16, 4:5, 1:1 as required), a clean opening within the first 3 seconds, captions on screen, raw files delivered, delivery format. Check the current safe zones in the placement preview.

## Hook direction
The opening idea and the persona it speaks to; alternates the creator may try. See `hook-writer`.

## Usage rights
- Where the content may be used: organic, paid, the brand's channels, whitelisting or partnership ads.
- **Duration** of the rights, with start and end dates.
- **Whether paid use is allowed**, and whether the brand may edit the content.
- Fee and any extra fee for an extension.

Put the rights in writing before the ad runs, and track the end date so the ad is switched off in time.

## Disclosure
The post must carry the platform's paid partnership or branded content label where its rules require it, and a clear disclosure such as #ad where the law or the platform asks. Follow the current rules for each platform and for the audience's country; check them before posting.

## Feedback rounds and dates
Number of feedback rounds, dates for the first draft, feedback, the final files and go-live. Who approves, and how long approval takes.
