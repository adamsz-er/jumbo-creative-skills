# Planning refreshes

## What to refresh first

1. **Fading ads that still pay.** Keep-or-kill calls them "Iterate": the concept earns, the execution is tiring. Refresh the opening or the creator and keep the idea.
2. **Ads on course to fade, soonest first.** Brief them early enough that the new version is live before the old one crosses its floor: the brief-to-live time is `--lead-days`.
3. **Fading ads that no longer pay.** These are pause candidates, not refreshes. A new version of an idea that stopped paying is a new test, so it belongs in `creative-ideation`, not the refresh queue.

The calendar orders fading ads by spend (most money exposed first) and on-course ads by their estimated fade date.

## What a refresh changes

Change one thing so the result says what worked:

| Change | When |
|---|---|
| The first seconds (spoken line, on-screen text, first frame) | Hook rate is falling; the ad stops fewer people |
| The creator or face | The idea holds but the audience has seen this person too often |
| The format (a video idea as a carousel, a static as a short video) | Click-through is falling across the ad's life, hook rate holds |
| The offer framing in the copy | Click-through holds but purchases fall: check the page first (`funnel-diagnosis`) |

`hook-writer` writes one-change variants; `creative-brief` turns each into a production brief.

## Reading the estimate

- It is a straight line through the ad's last few delivery days. Real ads often fade faster at first and then flatten, or hold and then drop. Treat the number as a planning date, not a prediction.
- The range is the line's slope plus and minus one standard error. A wide range means the recent days were noisy; a range with "no end in sight" means the line may not be falling at all.
- The floor is the lower quartile of the same rate among the account's ads of the same format. Crossing it means the ad has become one of the weaker ones of its kind, not that it has stopped working.
- Frequency rises as the same people see an ad again. A rising frequency with a falling click-through rate is the classic fatigue pattern; a falling rate with flat frequency can be a seasonal or audience change rather than fatigue.

## Sizing capacity

Count what the team can make in a normal week, not a good one. If the queue does not clear within the horizon, the honest options are more capacity, fewer live ads (so each gets enough delivery to tire more slowly), or accepting that some ads run past their floor. The calendar shows which.
