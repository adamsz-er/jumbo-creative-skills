# Persona template

Fill one per persona. Write it in plain language. Leave a field "unknown" rather than guessing. Mark what the user told you versus what you assumed.

```
Name (a label for the state, not a person): e.g. "The Weekend Doubter"

Emotional starting state: <how they feel before they find the product>
Emotional state they want: <how they want to feel after>
Awareness stage: unaware | problem-aware | solution-aware | product-aware | ready to buy

The job they hire the product for: <what they are trying to get done>
Trigger moments: <what happens that sends them looking>
Objections: <what makes them hesitate, in their words>
Proof they need: <what would make them believe it>
Language they use: <phrases they say, from reviews or conversations the user shared>
Where they are: <feed, search, a friend's recommendation, in store>

Light demographic note (optional, only if the user supplied it): <context, never a casting instruction>
Source: told by the user | from customer words the user shared | assumed
```

## How the two key fields work

**Emotional starting state** is the feeling the viewer carries into the feed. An ad that starts from the wrong feeling reads as noise: a reassuring ad aimed at someone who is excited misses; a hype ad aimed at someone who is anxious feels pushy. Name the start and the destination, and let the ad move the viewer from one to the other.

**Awareness stage** decides how much the ad must explain:

| Stage | They know | The ad must |
|---|---|---|
| Unaware | Nothing about the problem | Interrupt and make the problem visible |
| Problem-aware | The problem, not the options | Name it precisely and promise a way through |
| Solution-aware | The kind of solution | Show why this one is different |
| Product-aware | The product, not yet convinced | Remove the objection and add proof |
| Ready to buy | They want it | Make the next step and the offer clear |
