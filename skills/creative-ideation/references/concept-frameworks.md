# Concept frameworks

Ten ways into an idea. Pick the angle first, then choose how to make it. Each line says what the ad must show and the question it answers for the viewer.

| Angle | What it does | The ad must show | Question it answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Problem | Names a frustration the viewer already feels | The moment of the problem, in the viewer's words | "Is this about me?" |
| Proof | Shows the product doing the job | A real demonstration, test or result the brand can substantiate | "Does it work?" |
| Offer | Leads with a deal, bundle or guarantee | The offer and its dates, stated plainly | "What do I get, and until when?" |
| Emotion | Sells the feeling after the purchase | The before and after state, not the feature list | "How will I feel?" |
| Identity | Speaks to who the viewer is or wants to be | A recognisable group, habit or value | "Is this for people like me?" |
| Comparison | Sets the product against the old way or an alternative | The difference in one visible contrast; never name or disparage a competitor without legal sign-off | "Why this and not that?" |
| Behind the scenes | Shows how it is made or tested | The people, place or process | "Can I trust this?" |
| Founder story | Puts the reason the brand exists on camera | The founder, speaking plainly, with one concrete detail | "Who is behind this?" |
| Social proof | Lets buyers carry the story | Real reviews, real photos or real creators with permission | "Do people like me buy this?" |
| Education | Teaches something useful that leads to the product | One clear lesson, then where the product fits | "What will I learn?" |

## Turning an angle into a concept

1. Pick the angle and the persona's starting state (from `persona-builder`).
2. State the single message in one sentence.
3. Choose the format that makes the proof easiest to see (a demonstration wants video, a range wants a carousel).
4. Write the hook before the body: if the first moment is weak, change the idea, not the colour grade.
5. Write what would prove the concept wrong: the funnel step that should move, and what it would look like if it did not.

## Iterating a winner

When an ad is marked "iterate" or "scale", keep the concept and change exactly one thing: the opening, the creator, the setting, the length, the format. Label what changed so the result can be read. Several near-copies of one concept share one fatigue clock, so they are not a wide test.

## Adjacent moves

- **Adapt to an adjacent audience:** same concept, a different persona's starting state and proof.
- **Adapt to an adjacent product:** same concept, the next product in the range.
- **Genuinely new:** an angle the account has not tried; expect the widest spread of outcomes.
