# Sale calendar

Dates move year to year: work out each one for the year you plan, and confirm retailer-run events on their organiser's site.

## Australia

| Moment | When | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Valentine's Day | 14 February | Gifting; delivery cut-off copy in the last week |
| Easter | Varies (March or April) | Public holidays change delivery times |
| Mother's Day | Second Sunday in May | Gifting; one of the larger gifting moments |
| End of financial year | June, ending on the last day of June | Clearance and business purchases; "EOFY" sales run most of the month |
| Father's Day | First Sunday in September | Gifting |
| Retailer-run online shopping events | Usually May and November | Dates and participation vary: check the organiser |
| Singles' Day | 11 November | Larger in some categories and audiences than others |
| Black Friday | The Friday after the fourth Thursday in November | Now a major Australian sale moment; often a week or more of trading |
| Cyber Monday | The Monday after Black Friday | Often run as one event with Black Friday |
| Christmas | December | Gifting phases: early, peak, last-minute (fast shipping, gift cards); publish delivery cut-offs |
| Boxing Day | 26 December | Clearance; often runs into January |

## Your market

Ask the user for their market's moments and add them as rows: the date rule, the type (gifting, clearance, event), and any public holidays that change delivery or attention. Check the market's own rules on sale claims before planning copy.

## Around each moment

- Count back from the event for the teaser and early-access phases (`sale-phases.md`).
- Put creative briefing and approvals before the teaser starts.
- Plan the post-sale weeks too: size their targets from the account's own pull-forward read on its last comparable sale (`scripts/sale_lift.py`, see `sale-phases.md`), and have full-price creative ready.
